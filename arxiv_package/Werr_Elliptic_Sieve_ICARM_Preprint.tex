\begin{abstract}
The computational verification of high-rank elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ has historically been bottlenecked by the difficulty of certifying global arithmetic invariants, most notably the arithmetic conductor $N$. In modern automated reasoning platforms such as the NSF Institute for Computer-Aided Reasoning in Mathematics (ICARM) Elliptic Curve Rank Leaderboard (maintained at Carnegie Mellon University under U.S.\ National Science Foundation Grant DMS-2425401), candidate curves are subjected to strict client-side and in-worker WebAssembly PARI/GP 2.17.2 kernel verification. While geometric non-singularity, rational point membership, torsion subgroup structures, and N\'{e}ron--Tate height regulator determinants are verified efficiently, computing the global conductor via Tate's algorithm (\texttt{ellglobalred}) requires the complete prime factorization of the minimal discriminant $\Delta$. For state-of-the-art curves specializing high-rank parametric families, $|\Delta|$ routinely spans 50 to 110 decimal digits, leaving many landmark curves across the literature with uncertified conductors (marked as \texttt{?} or unranked).

In this paper, we present an automated certificate-generation and isogeny graph exploration framework that resolves this bottleneck. Our methodology unites two engines tailored specifically to the arithmetic of elliptic curves: (1) the \emph{4-Stage Werr Multi-Layer Algebraic Sieve}, which couples Stage 1 algebraic invariant-root and division-polynomial gcd kernels ($\gcd(c_4, c_6, \Delta, a_4, a_6)$), Stage 2 elliptic syzygy and monic cubic divisor peeling ($c_4^3 - 1728\Delta = c_6^2$, $\Delta = d_0 \cdot s^3$, and 2-torsion pairwise root-difference decomposition), Stage 3 parallel Pollard $\rho$, Pollard $p-1$, and multi-curve Montgomery/Weierstrass ECM ($B_1 = 10^5, B_2 = 5 \times 10^6$), and Stage 4 word-packed $\mathbb{F}_2$ Multipolynomial Quadratic Sieve (MPQS) with deterministic APR-CL / Miller--Rabin primality closure; and (2) systematic \emph{V\'{e}lu Isogeny Graph Transport}, traversing rational $n$-isogeny neighborhoods ($n \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12\}$) and exact point-interpolation Weierstrass recovery to discover minimal models with strictly reduced discriminants, Faltings heights, and naive heights while preserving Mordell--Weil rank and conductor.

Deploying this pipeline on the NSF ICARM leaderboard, we established \textbf{230 newly certified curves} (\texttt{\#4308}--\texttt{\#4537}) across 8 rational torsion subgroups with \textbf{100\% certified conductor factorizations} (230/230, eliminating all missing conductor certificates), securing \textbf{30 \#1 Gold records}, \textbf{80 Top-3 podium finishes}, and \textbf{418 Top-10 placements} across 43 active leaderboards. Landmark results include the ``Grand Slam'' curve \texttt{\#4315} ($T = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathrm{Rank} \ge 9$), which captures \texttt{\#1} in all four metrics simultaneously, the factorization of a 110-digit discriminant into 21 distinct bad primes for the Rank $\ge 12$ curve \texttt{\#4383}, and full conductor certification ($\log N = 141.86$) for the Elkies Rank $\ge 19$ curve \texttt{\#4323}. All datasets, reproduction scripts, and verification receipts are permanently archived at CERN Zenodo (DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23436282}{10.5281/zenodo.23436282}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Computer-Aided Reasoning in Arithmetic Geometry}

The interface between automated deduction, formal proof verification, and computational number theory has undergone a profound transformation over the past decade \cite{avigad2021formal,blanchette2016interactive,hales2017formal}. Historically, computational records in arithmetic geometry---such as tables of elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with high Mordell--Weil rank or prescribed rational torsion subgroups---were compiled as static text repositories maintained by individual researchers. While foundational to number theory, static tables rely on implicit trust in unbundled off-line scripts and frequently omit computationally expensive global invariants.

A major advance toward interactive, machine-certified mathematical benchmarking is the \textbf{NSF Institute for Computer-Aided Reasoning in Mathematics (ICARM)}, established at Carnegie Mellon University and partner institutions under U.S.\ National Science Foundation Grant DMS-2425401. As part of its automated reasoning infrastructure, ICARM operates the official \textbf{Elliptic Curve Rank Leaderboard} (\url{https://elliptic-rank.icarm.cloud}), architected and maintained by David Renshaw and Seewoo Lee \cite{icarm2026leaderboard}. Rather than accepting unverified user assertions, the ICARM platform enforces a zero-trust, certificate-driven verification protocol executed inside an isolated WebAssembly (WASM) worker running the computer algebra kernel \textbf{PARI/GP 2.17.2} \cite{parigp2024}.

\subsection{The In-Worker WebAssembly Verification Protocol}

When a contributor submits a candidate elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ defined by integer Weierstrass coefficients $\mathbf{a} = [a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4, a_6]$, a list of rational point coordinates $\mathcal{P} = \{P_1, \dots, P_r\}$, and an optional list of prime factors $\mathcal{S} = \{p_1, \dots, p_k\}$, the ICARM verification kernel executes a six-stage formal decision procedure:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item \textbf{Geometric Non-Singularity Check:} The kernel evaluates the Tate invariants $b_2, b_4, b_6, b_8$, the covariants $c_4, c_6$, and the discriminant $\Delta(\mathbf{a})$. It verifies that $\Delta(\mathbf{a}) \neq 0$, guaranteeing that $E$ defines a smooth projective curve of genus $1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
    \item \textbf{Canonical Isomorphism Key Generation:} Using LMFDB-compatible invariant reduction, the kernel removes admissible scaling powers $u = 2^\alpha 3^\beta$ from $(c_4, c_6)$ to compute the canonical isomorphism class key \texttt{"c4:c6"}, preventing duplicate submissions of isomorphic models.
    \item \textbf{Exact Rational Point Membership:} For every asserted generator $P_j = (x_j, y_j) \in \mathbb{Q}^2$, the kernel verifies exact arithmetic equality in $\mathbb{Q}$:
    \begin{equation}
    y_j^2 + a_1 x_j y_j + a_3 y_j = x_j^3 + a_2 x_j^2 + a_4 x_j + a_6.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Torsion Subgroup Certification:} Applying Lutz--Nagell bounds and division polynomial root analysis via \texttt{elltors(E)}, the kernel certifies the exact rational torsion subgroup $E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}} \cong T$ among the 15 admissible groups classified by Mazur's Torsion Theorem \cite{mazur1977modular}.
    \item \textbf{Mordell--Weil Rank Certification via Regulator Positivity:} The kernel computes the canonical N\'{e}ron--Tate height pairing matrix
    \begin{equation}
    R(\mathcal{P}) = \left( \langle P_i, P_j \rangle \right)_{1 \le i,j \le r}, \quad \langle P_i, P_j \rangle = \hat{h}(P_i + P_j) - \hat{h}(P_i) - \hat{h}(P_j),
    \end{equation}
    using \texttt{ellbil(E, P\_i, P\_j)} at high precision, and extracts a maximal $\mathbb{Z}$-linearly independent subset of $\mathcal{P}$ whose Gram determinant $\det R > 0$ is strictly bounded away from zero. This provides a machine-certified lower bound $\mathrm{rank}(E(\mathbb{Q})) \ge r$.
    \item \textbf{Global Minimal Reduction and Conductor Certification ($\log N$):} Finally, the kernel invokes Tate's global reduction algorithm \cite{tate1975algorithm} via \texttt{ellglobalred(E)} to transform $E$ to its global minimal integral Weierstrass equation, compute the minimal discriminant $|\Delta_{\min}|$, the Faltings height $h_F(E)$, the naive height $h_{\text{naive}}(E)$, and the global arithmetic conductor $N$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Conductor Bottleneck on High-Rank Leaderboards}

Across the ICARM leaderboard, curves within each $(T, r)$ category---where $T = E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}$ is the torsion subgroup and $r$ is the certified Mordell--Weil rank lower bound---are ranked across four primary minimality metrics:
\begin{equation}
\text{(i) Conductor } N, \;\; \text{(ii) Discriminant } |\Delta|, \;\; \text{(iii) Faltings Height } h_F(E), \;\; \text{(iv) Naive Height } h_{\text{naive}}(E).
\end{equation}
While $|\Delta|$, $h_F(E)$, and $h_{\text{naive}}(E)$ can be evaluated directly from the Weierstrass coefficients once local scaling factors at $p \in \{2, 3\}$ are removed, computing the conductor $N = \prod_{p \mid \Delta} p^{f_p}$ requires knowing the \emph{complete prime factorization} of the discriminant $\Delta$.

In high-rank elliptic curve constructions pioneered by Noam Elkies \cite{elkies2006high}, Andrej Dujella \cite{dujella2024highrank}, Michael Rubinstein \cite{rubinstein2024tables}, and Zev Klagsbrun, curves of rank $r \in [7, 29]$ are obtained by specializing multi-parameter K3 and rational elliptic surfaces over $\mathbb{Q}(t_1, \dots, t_m)$ at rational parameters of large height. Because the discriminant $\Delta$ is a polynomial of high degree in the specialization parameters, the resulting integer $|\Delta|$ routinely spans \textbf{50 to 110 decimal digits} (165 to 365 bits). When submitted without a pre-computed prime factorization certificate $\mathcal{S}$, the in-worker PARI/GP kernel cannot factor $|\Delta|$ within the interactive execution budget, leaving the conductor field marked as \texttt{?} (unfactored/unknown) and disqualifying the curve from the Conductor $N$ leaderboard.

Furthermore, many high-rank curves in the historical literature are recorded in a single Weierstrass coordinate system tied to their fibration parameterization, without exploring the finite graph of $\mathbb{Q}$-isogenous models. Because rational isogenies preserve both the Mordell--Weil rank $r$ and the conductor $N$ while altering $|\Delta|$, $h_F(E)$, and $h_{\text{naive}}(E)$, uninspected vertices of the rational isogeny graph often conceal strictly smaller minimal models.

\subsection{Summary of Contributions}

In this work, we bridge automated proof-certificate generation and computational arithmetic geometry through three primary contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item \textbf{The 4-Stage Werr Multi-Layer Algebraic Sieve for Elliptic Discriminants:} We design a specialized four-stage factorization and certificate synthesis pipeline that exploits Weierstrass covariant gcds, elliptic syzygy cubic peeling ($\Delta = d_0 \cdot s^3$), 2-torsion monic cubic root-difference splitting, parallel Pollard $\rho$/$p-1$ and multi-curve ECM, and word-packed $\mathbb{F}_2$ MPQS closure to completely factor composite discriminants up to 110 decimal digits.
    \item \textbf{Systematic V\'{e}lu Isogeny Graph Transport and Point-Interpolation Recovery:} We implement an exact rational isogeny graph explorer covering subgroups of orders $n \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12\}$ together with an exact linear point-interpolation solver that reconstructs missing Weierstrass models from historical point tables and transports Mordell--Weil generators $P_j \mapsto \phi(P_j)$ across 2- and 3-isogeny graphs.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Public Certification on the NSF ICARM Leaderboard:} We submitted and certified \textbf{230 distinct Top-10 curves} (\texttt{\#4308} through \texttt{\#4537}) under contributor profile \texttt{user/114} (\url{https://elliptic-rank.icarm.cloud/user/114}), achieving \textbf{100.0\% conductor certification} ($230/230$ with zero unknown conductors), \textbf{30 \#1 Gold records}, \textbf{80 Top-3 podiums}, and \textbf{418 Top-10 metric placements} across 43 active leaderboards \cite{dagli2026zenodo}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conductor Determination as Formal Proof Certificate Checking}

Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve defined by a generalized Weierstrass equation with integer coefficients:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weierstrass}
E: \quad y^2 + a_1 x y + a_3 y = x^3 + a_2 x^2 + a_4 x + a_6, \quad a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4, a_6 \in \mathbb{Z}.
\end{equation}
The standard Tate quantities $b_2, b_4, b_6, b_8$ and covariants $c_4, c_6, \Delta$ are given by:
\begin{align}
b_2 &= a_1^2 + 4a_2, \qquad b_4 = 2a_4 + a_1 a_3, \qquad b_6 = a_3^2 + 4a_6, \\
b_8 &= a_1^2 a_6 + 4a_2 a_6 - a_1 a_3 a_4 + a_2 a_3^2 - a_4^2, \\
c_4 &= b_2^2 - 24b_4, \qquad c_6 = -b_2^3 + 36b_2 b_4 - 216b_6, \\
\Delta &= -b_2^2 b_8 - 8b_4^3 - 27b_6^2 + 9b_2 b_4 b_6 = \frac{c_4^3 - c_6^2}{1728}.
\end{align}
An admissible change of Weierstrass coordinates over $\mathbb{Q}$ takes the form $(x, y) = (u^2 x' + r, u^3 y' + u^2 s x' + t)$ with $u \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ and $r, s, t \in \mathbb{Q}$, under which the invariants transform as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:u_scaling}
c_4' = u^{-4} c_4, \qquad c_6' = u^{-6} c_6, \qquad \Delta' = u^{-12} \Delta.
\end{equation}
An integral model~\eqref{eq:weierstrass} is \emph{globally minimal} over $\mathbb{Z}$ if $v_p(\Delta)$ is minimized simultaneously at every prime $p$. Because $\mathbb{Z}$ has class number $1$, every elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ admits a unique normalized global minimal model satisfying $a_1, a_3 \in \{0, 1\}$ and $a_2 \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$.

\begin{definition}[Arithmetic Conductor and Ogg--Saito Formula]
Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be a globally minimal Weierstrass model with minimal discriminant $\Delta_{\min}$. The \emph{arithmetic conductor} of $E$ is the positive integer
\begin{equation}
N(E) = \prod_{p \mid \Delta_{\min}} p^{f_p},
\end{equation}
where the local exponent $f_p \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ is given by the Ogg--Saito formula:
\begin{equation}
f_p = v_p(\Delta_{\min}) - m_p + 1,
\end{equation}
with $m_p$ denoting the number of irreducible components (counted without multiplicity) of the special fiber $\mathcal{E}_p$ of the N\'{e}ron minimal model of $E$ over $\mathbb{Z}_p$. Specifically:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item $f_p = 0$ if and only if $p \nmid \Delta_{\min}$ ($E$ has good reduction at $p$, Kodaira type $\mathrm{I}_0$);
    \item $f_p = 1$ if and only if $p \mid \Delta_{\min}$ and $p \nmid c_4$ ($E$ has multiplicative reduction at $p$, Kodaira type $\mathrm{I}_n$ with $n = v_p(\Delta_{\min}) \ge 1$);
    \item $f_p = 2 + \delta_p \ge 2$ if $E$ has additive reduction at $p$ ($p \mid \gcd(c_4, \Delta_{\min})$), where the wild Swan ramification invariant $\delta_p = 0$ for all primes $p \ge 5$, while $0 \le \delta_3 \le 3$ ($f_3 \le 5$) and $0 \le \delta_2 \le 6$ ($f_2 \le 8$).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Certificate Verification Complexity of Tate's Algorithm]\label{prop:tate_complexity}
Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be specified by an integral Weierstrass tuple $\mathbf{a} = [a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4, a_6] \in \mathbb{Z}^5$ with discriminant $\Delta \neq 0$. Suppose an external prover supplies a prime factorization certificate
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S} = \{p_1, p_2, \dots, p_k\} \quad \text{such that} \quad |\Delta| = \prod_{i=1}^k p_i^{e_i}, \quad e_i = v_{p_i}(\Delta) \ge 1.
\end{equation}
Then a verification kernel can certify the primality of each $p_i$, execute Kraus--Laska--Connell local minimization at every $p_i$ with $e_i \ge 12$, run Tate's algorithm \cite{tate1975algorithm} at all bad primes, and output the exact global minimal model $E_{\min}$ and certified conductor $N(E)$ in deterministic time polynomial in $\log |\Delta|$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Verification of the identity $|\Delta| = \prod_{i=1}^k p_i^{e_i}$ requires at most $\sum_{i=1}^k e_i \le \log_2 |\Delta|$ multi-precision divisions of bit-length $B = \lceil \log_2 |\Delta| \rceil$, costing $O(B^2)$ bit operations. Primality verification of each $p_i \le |\Delta|$ via deterministic APR-CL \cite{cohen1984primality} or ECPP certificates runs in time $O((\log p_i)^{4+\varepsilon})$. For each prime $p_i \in \mathcal{S}$, Tate's algorithm \cite{cremona1997algorithms,tate1975algorithm} executes at most $\lfloor e_i / 12 \rfloor + 1$ local loop iterations. Each iteration computes $p_i$-adic valuations of the invariants and finds roots over $\mathbb{F}_{p_i}$ of auxiliary polynomials of degree at most $3$:
\begin{align}
P_1(T) &= T^2 + a_1 T - a_2 \pmod{p_i}, \\
P_2(Y) &= Y^2 + a_3 Y - a_6 \pmod{p_i}, \\
P_3(X) &= X^3 + a_{2,1} X^2 + a_{4,2} X + a_{6,3} \pmod{p_i}, \quad a_{j,m} = p_i^{-m} a_j.
\end{align}
Root-finding for degree $\le 3$ polynomials over $\mathbb{F}_{p_i}$ requires $O(\log^2 p_i)$ field operations via Cantor--Zassenhaus. Since $k \le \log_2 |\Delta|$ and $\sum_{i=1}^k \log p_i \le \log |\Delta|$, the entire global reduction and conductor certification procedure terminates in $O(\mathrm{poly}(\log |\Delta|))$ time.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Automated Reasoning Architecture: Separating Search from Verification]
Proposition~\ref{prop:tate_complexity} exemplifies the foundational design principle of modern computer-aided reasoning (\texttt{cs.LO}): \emph{separating untrusted, computationally intensive certificate search from lightweight, deterministic kernel verification}. In the ICARM architecture, the submission payload includes the field \texttt{"primes": ["p\_1", ..., "p\_k"]}. Crucially, if a submitted model $\mathbf{a}$ is non-minimal with scaling factor $u \in \mathbb{Z}_{>1}$, its raw discriminant satisfies $\Delta(\mathbf{a}) = u^{12} \Delta_{\min}$. For PARI/GP's in-worker \texttt{ellglobalred} routine to succeed without factoring timeouts, the certificate $\mathcal{S}$ must cover all prime divisors of the \emph{submitted} discriminant $\Delta(\mathbf{a})$ (including primes dividing $u$). Once $\mathcal{S}$ is registered via \texttt{addprimes(S)}, the WebAssembly worker verifies trial-division completeness against $\Delta(\mathbf{a})$, strips $u^{12}$, and certifies $\log N$ in under 2 seconds.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Stage 1: Algebraic Root and Covariant GCD Kernel}

Before invoking probabilistic or sub-exponential factoring routines, Stage 1 exploits the polynomial dependencies among the Weierstrass coefficients and Tate covariants. Specifically, Stage 1 strips all small primes $p \le 10^5$ via a fast byte-sieve wheel (\texttt{SMALL\_PRIMES}) and computes the mutual greatest common divisors:
\begin{equation}
g_1 = \gcd(|c_4|, |c_6|), \quad g_2 = \gcd(|a_4|, |a_6|), \quad g_3 = \gcd(|b_2|, |b_4|, |b_6|, |b_8|), \quad g_4 = \gcd(|\Delta|, |c_4|, |c_6|).
\end{equation}
Whenever $g_k > 1$, Euclidean gcd splitting immediately factors $\Delta$ into smaller coprime components $\Delta / g_k^m$ and $g_k$. Furthermore, when $E$ possesses non-trivial rational torsion, the division polynomials $\psi_n(x)$ impose exact polynomial factorizations on $\Delta$:

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic Discriminant Decomposition via Rational 2-Torsion]\label{prop:cubic_peeling}
Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an integral Weierstrass model possessing at least one rational 2-torsion point $P_0 = (x_0, y_0) \in E(\mathbb{Q})[2] \setminus \{O\}$. Then $X_0 = 4x_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$ is an integer root of the monic cubic polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monic_cubic}
f(X) = X^3 + B_2 X^2 + B_4 X + B_6 = 0, \quad B_2 = b_2, \quad B_4 = 8b_4, \quad B_6 = 16b_6 \in \mathbb{Z}.
\end{equation}
Factoring $f(X) = (X - X_0)(X^2 + \alpha X + \beta)$ with $\alpha = B_2 + X_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $\beta = B_4 + \alpha X_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$ decomposes the elliptic discriminant $\Delta(E)$ algebraically into the product:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta_peel}
\Delta(E) = 2^{-12} \cdot (\alpha^2 - 4\beta) \cdot (3X_0^2 + 2B_2 X_0 + B_4)^2.
\end{equation}
In particular, if $E(\mathbb{Q})[2] \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ with three integer roots $X_0, X_1, X_2$ of $f(X)$, then $\Delta(E)$ splits completely into the square of the pairwise root differences:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta_full_2tors}
\Delta(E) = 2^{-12} \cdot (X_0 - X_1)^2 (X_0 - X_2)^2 (X_1 - X_2)^2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A point $P_0 = (x_0, y_0)$ satisfies $2P_0 = O$ if and only if $2y_0 + a_1 x_0 + a_3 = 0$. Completing the square in the Weierstrass equation~\eqref{eq:weierstrass} yields $(2y + a_1 x + a_3)^2 = 4x^3 + b_2 x^2 + 2b_4 x + b_6$. Multiplying by $16$ and substituting $X = 4x$ produces the monic integer polynomial $f(X) = X^3 + B_2 X^2 + B_4 X + B_6$. By Gauss's Lemma, any rational root of $f(X)$ is an integer $X_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$. The polynomial discriminant of $f(X)$ is $\mathrm{Disc}(f) = 2^{12} \Delta(E)$. For a factored polynomial $f(X) = (X - X_0) q(X)$, the resultant identity $\mathrm{Disc}(f) = \mathrm{Disc}(q) \cdot q(X_0)^2 = (\alpha^2 - 4\beta) (f'(X_0))^2$ yields~\eqref{eq:delta_peel} and~\eqref{eq:delta_full_2tors}.
\end{proof}

Crucially, finding the integer roots $X_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$ of $f(X)$ requires \emph{zero integer factorization}: since $f(X)$ is a cubic with at most two real local extrema, Stage 1 computes the overflow-safe bit-length bound $M = 2^{\max(\mathrm{bits}(B_2), \mathrm{bits}(B_4)/2, \mathrm{bits}(B_6)/3) + 4}$ and locates all integer roots in $O(\log |B_6|)$ arithmetic operations via exact integer bisection (\texttt{integer\_roots\_monic\_cubic}). For curves with full 2-torsion $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ (such as the 110-digit discriminant curve \texttt{\#4383}), identity~\eqref{eq:delta_full_2tors} immediately reduces the problem of factoring a 110-digit integer $|\Delta|$ to factoring three integers $|X_i - X_j|$ of at most 18--20 digits each!

\subsection{Stage 2: Elliptic Syzygy and Cubic Divisor Peeling}

For curves with trivial 2-torsion ($E(\mathbb{Q})[2] = \{O\}$), Stage 2 exploits the fundamental elliptic syzygy:
\begin{equation}
c_4^3 - 1728 \Delta = c_6^2 \implies c_4^3 \equiv c_6^2 \pmod{1728 \Delta}.
\end{equation}
Any prime $p \ge 5$ dividing both $c_4$ and $\Delta$ (primes of additive reduction) satisfies $p^3 \mid c_4^3$ and $p^2 \mid c_6^2$, allowing Stage 2 to isolate cubic and square divisor factors $\Delta = d_0 \cdot s^3$ via iterated $\gcd(\Delta, c_4^3)$ and exact integer root extraction $\mathtt{isqrt}(curr)$. Similarly, when an unreduced or V\'{e}lu-transported model has $\Delta_{\text{sub}} = u^{12} \Delta_{\min}$, Stage 2 peels the 12th-power scaling factor $u^{12}$ by computing $\gcd(c_4^3, c_6^2, \Delta)$.

\subsection{Stage 3: Parallel Pollard $\rho$, Pollard $p-1$, and Multi-Curve ECM}

Each remaining composite cofactor $C > 1$ (after checking deterministic primality via \texttt{is\_probable\_prime} and exact square peeling \texttt{isqrt}) is pushed onto a factorization work-stack and processed by Stage 3:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item \textbf{Batched Pollard--Brent $\rho$:} Workers execute Brent's cycle-finding improvement to Pollard's $\rho$ algorithm with polynomial transition $x \mapsto (x^2 + c) \bmod C$, accumulating modular products in batches of $128$ steps before computing $\gcd(\prod |x_i - y_i|, C)$ up to $2.5 \times 10^5$ iterations. This rapidly strips all prime factors up to $14$--$16$ decimal digits.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Curve Weierstrass/Montgomery ECM (\texttt{ecm\_stage1}):} For cofactors containing $15$ to $32$ digit primes, Stage 3 launches parallel Lenstra Elliptic Curve Method (ECM) workers \cite{lenstra1987factoring}. Each worker generates random curves $E_a: y^2 = x^3 + ax + b \pmod C$ passing through a random initial point $P_0 = (x_0, y_0) \bmod C$ by setting $b \equiv y_0^2 - x_0^3 - a x_0 \pmod C$, checks $\gcd(4a^3 + 27b^2, C)$, and multiplies $P_0$ by prime powers $p^k \le B_1$ ($B_1 = 10^5$, Stage 2 bound $B_2 = 5 \times 10^6$). Whenever a projective denominator $2y_Q$ or $(x_Q - x_R)$ shares a non-trivial factor with $C$, the modular inverse exception $\gcd(\mathrm{den}, C) \in (1, C)$ immediately yields a prime factor of $C$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 4: Word-Packed $\mathbb{F}_2$ MPQS and PARI/GP 2.17.2 Closure}

When a composite cofactor $C$ of $60$ to $130$ bits resists ECM (typically a balanced semiprime product of two $18$ to $22$ digit primes, as in the 74-digit discriminant of the Rank $\ge 19$ curve \texttt{\#4323}), Stage 4 invokes our Word-Packed $\mathbb{F}_2$ Multipolynomial Quadratic Sieve (\texttt{solve\_word\_packed\_f2\_qs}) and PARI/GP 2.17.2 MPQS closure:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item Polynomials $Q(x) = (Ax + B)^2 - C$ are sieved over the factor base $\mathcal{B} = \{p \le B_{\max} : (\frac{C}{p}) = 1\}$ using Tonelli--Shanks modular square roots.
    \item Smooth exponent vectors modulo $2$ are packed into 64-bit machine words (\texttt{uint64}), and $\mathbb{F}_2$-nullspace dependencies are extracted via bitwise MSB-pivot Gaussian elimination to construct $X^2 \equiv Y^2 \pmod C$, yielding $\gcd(X - Y, C) = p$.
    \item Every extracted factor is certified prime via deterministic Miller--Rabin / Baillie--PSW and APR-CL primality tests, producing the sorted bad-prime certificate $\mathcal{S} = \{p_1, \dots, p_k\}$ ready for ICARM API submission.
\end{itemize}

\section{V\'{e}lu Rational Isogeny Graph Transport and Minimal Model Search}

While the 4-Stage Werr Algebraic Sieve resolves the conductor certification bottleneck ($\log N$), our second engine exploits the geometry of rational isogeny graphs to discover strictly smaller minimal Weierstrass models and populate adjacent torsion leaderboards.

\subsection{Invariance of Rank and Conductor Across $\mathbb{Q}$-Isogeny Orbits}

Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and $E'/\mathbb{Q}$ be elliptic curves connected by a non-constant $\mathbb{Q}$-rational isogeny $\phi: E \to E'$ of degree $n = |\ker \phi|$. Let $\hat{\phi}: E' \to E$ denote the dual isogeny satisfying $\hat{\phi} \circ \phi = [n]_E$ and $\phi \circ \hat{\phi} = [n]_{E'}$.

\begin{theorem}[Invariance of Rank, Conductor, and $L$-Function under $\mathbb{Q}$-Isogeny]\label{thm:isogeny_invariance}
Let $\phi: E \to E'$ be a $\mathbb{Q}$-rational isogeny between elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Mordell--Weil Rank Invariance:} $\phi$ induces a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear isomorphism $E(\mathbb{Q}) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} \xrightarrow{\sim} E'(\mathbb{Q}) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$, so $\mathrm{rank}(E'(\mathbb{Q})) = \mathrm{rank}(E(\mathbb{Q}))$.
    \item \textbf{Conductor and Bad Prime Invariance:} By the N\'{e}ron--Ogg--Shafarevich criterion and Tate's isogeny theorem, the $\ell$-adic Galois representations $V_\ell(E) \cong V_\ell(E')$ are isomorphic as $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$-modules. Consequently, $E$ and $E'$ have the exact same set of bad primes $\mathcal{S}$, the same global arithmetic conductor $N(E') = N(E)$, and identical Hasse--Weil $L$-functions $L(E', s) = L(E, s)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Crucially, however, the \emph{minimal discriminant} $|\Delta(E')|$, the \emph{Faltings height} $h_F(E')$, and the \emph{naive height} $h_{\text{naive}}(E') = \log \max(|c_4|^3, c_6^2)$ are \textbf{not} invariant under isogeny! For a prime $\ell$-isogeny $\phi: E \to E'$, a multiplicative fiber of Kodaira type $\mathrm{I}_m$ at a prime $p \mid N$ transitions either to $\mathrm{I}_{m \cdot \ell}$ or to $\mathrm{I}_{m / \ell}$, multiplying or dividing $|\Delta_{\min}|$ by $\ell^m$ across multiple primes while shifting the Faltings height via Faltings' isogeny degree formula.

\subsection{Explicit V\'{e}lu Quotient Equations and Generator Point Transport}

Let $G \subset E(\mathbb{Q})$ be a finite $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$-stable subgroup of order $n$. Following V\'{e}lu \cite{velu1971isogenies}, we partition the non-zero elements $G \setminus \{O\} = G_2 \sqcup G_+ \sqcup G_-$, where $G_2 = \{Q \in G \setminus \{O\} : 2Q = O\}$ consists of the 2-torsion points in $G$, and each inverse pair $\{Q, -Q\}$ of order $> 2$ has one representative in $G_+$ and one in $G_-$. Setting $S = G_2 \sqcup G_+$, for each $Q = (x_Q, y_Q) \in S$ we compute in exact rational arithmetic (\texttt{fractions.Fraction}):
\begin{align}
g_Q^x &= 3 x_Q^2 + 2 a_2 x_Q + a_4 - a_1 y_Q, \qquad g_Q^y = -2 y_Q - a_1 x_Q - a_3, \\
t_Q &= \begin{cases} g_Q^x & \text{if } Q \in G_2, \\ 2 g_Q^x - a_1 g_Q^y & \text{if } Q \in G_+, \end{cases} \qquad u_Q = (g_Q^y)^2, \qquad w_Q = u_Q + x_Q t_Q.
\end{align}
Summing $t = \sum_{Q \in S} t_Q$ and $w = \sum_{Q \in S} w_Q$, the quotient curve $E' = E/G$ is given by the Weierstrass model $\mathbf{A} = [A_1, A_2, A_3, A_4, A_6]$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:velu_coeffs}
A_1 = a_1, \quad A_2 = a_2, \quad A_3 = a_3, \quad A_4 = a_4 - 5t, \quad A_6 = a_6 - b_2 t - 7w.
\end{equation}
Simultaneously, for every independent Mordell--Weil generator $P_j = (x_j, y_j) \in \mathcal{P}$ on $E(\mathbb{Q})$, our engine transports $P_j$ across the isogeny via the exact rational map $\phi(P_j) = (X_j, Y_j) \in E'(\mathbb{Q})$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:velu_transport}
X_j = x_j + \sum_{Q \in G \setminus \{O\}} \left( x(P_j + Q) - x(Q) \right), \qquad Y_j = y_j + \sum_{Q \in G \setminus \{O\}} \left( y(P_j + Q) - y(Q) \right).
\end{equation}
When a rational subgroup $G$ of order $3$ is generated by a root $x_0 \in \mathbb{Q}$ of the 3-division polynomial $\psi_3(x) = 3x^4 + b_2 x^3 + 3b_4 x^2 + 3b_6 x + b_8 = 0$ whose $y$-coordinate $y_0$ lies in a quadratic extension $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$, note that $t_Q = 2g_Q^x - a_1 g_Q^y$ and $u_Q = (g_Q^y)^2$ depend only on $x_0$ and $(2y_0 + a_1 x_0 + a_3)^2 = 4x_0^3 + b_2 x_0^2 + 2b_4 x_0 + b_6 \in \mathbb{Q}$! Thus, our 3-isogeny engine (\texttt{velu\_3isogeny\_from\_x0}) evaluates both $E/G$ and the transported rational points $\phi(P_j)$ purely over $\mathbb{Q}$ without ever instantiating $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$.

When the coordinates of $Q \in G \setminus \{O\}$ have rational denominators (e.g., $x_0 = X_0 / 4$), our engine clears denominators via the minimal scaling factor $u = 2^\alpha 3^\beta$ such that
\begin{equation}
\tilde{A}_1 = u A_1, \quad \tilde{A}_2 = u^2 A_2, \quad \tilde{A}_3 = u^3 A_3, \quad \tilde{A}_4 = u^4 A_4, \quad \tilde{A}_6 = u^6 A_6 \in \mathbb{Z},
\end{equation}
and scales every transported generator accordingly via $(\tilde{X}_j, \tilde{Y}_j) = (u^2 X_j, u^3 Y_j)$.

\subsection{Point-Interpolation Recovery and Cross-Torsion Isogeny Transitions}

In several archival web pages (such as Dujella's historical subpages \texttt{z2z2old*.html} and \texttt{z6old*.html}), high-rank records are listed only by their independent generator points $\{P_1, \dots, P_r\}$ without explicit Weierstrass coefficients $[a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4, a_6]$. Our pipeline recovers the exact Weierstrass equation by solving the linear system over $\mathbb{Q}$:
\begin{equation}
y_j x_j a_1 - x_j^2 a_2 + y_j a_3 - x_j a_4 - a_6 = x_j^3 - y_j^2 \quad (1 \le j \le r),
\end{equation}
verifying that all $r$ points lie on the reconstructed curve, and then expanding the curve across its rational 2- and 3-isogeny graph. \section{Scientific Impact: Resolving the $\log N$ Upper-Bound Gap}

A central problem in analytic and algebraic number theory---investigated extensively by Goldfeld, Mestre, Brumer, Watkins, and Elkies \cite{cremona1997algorithms,elkies2006high,silverman1986arithmetic}---is determining the asymptotic and explicit upper bounds for the Mordell--Weil rank $r = \mathrm{rank}(E(\mathbb{Q}))$ in terms of the analytic conductor $\log N(E)$. Under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) and the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture, Mestre's explicit formula bounds the analytic rank by $r = O(\log N / \log \log N)$, while Szpiro's conjecture posits that for any $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists a constant $C(\varepsilon)$ such that
\begin{equation}
|\Delta_{\min}(E)| \le C(\varepsilon) \cdot N(E)^{6 + \varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
Empirical testing of explicit Mestre--Brumer conductor bounds and Szpiro ratios $\sigma(E) = \log |\Delta_{\min}(E)| / \log N(E)$ at extreme ranks ($r \in [8, 19]$) has historically been hampered by the ``unfactored conductor (\texttt{?})'' gap: because factoring 70--110 digit discriminants is computationally expensive, high-rank curves were frequently deposited with known $|\Delta_{\min}|$ but unknown $N(E)$.

By delivering \textbf{100\% certified prime factorizations ($230/230$)} across all 230 high-rank curves in our dataset, this work provides a machine-checked empirical benchmark for arithmetic geometers:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item On the $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathrm{Rank} \ge 9$ Grand Slam curve \texttt{\#4315}, the Szpiro ratio is $\sigma(E_{4315}) = \log |\Delta| / \log N = 105.78 / 50.40 = 2.099$, and the rank-to-conductor ratio is $r / \log_{10} N = 9 / 21.89 = 0.411$.
    \item On the 110-digit discriminant curve \texttt{\#4383} ($\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \mathrm{Rank} \ge 12$), Stage 1--2 cubic peeling reveals that heavy prime powers at small primes drop the 110-digit discriminant ($|\Delta| \approx 1.37 \times 10^{109}$) to a 48-digit conductor ($N \approx 7.34 \times 10^{47}$), yielding a Szpiro ratio of $\sigma(E_{4383}) = 251.30 / 110.22 = 2.280$.
    \item On the Elkies $\mathrm{Rank} \ge 19$ curve \texttt{\#4323}, the certified conductor $\log N = 141.86$ ($\log_{10} N = 61.61$) establishes an explicit certified high-rank benchmark point with Szpiro ratio $\sigma(E_{4323}) = 168.86 / 141.86 = 1.190$.
\end{itemize}
